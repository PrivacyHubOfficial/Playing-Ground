# 3ve Botnet — Understanding It from First Principles

The 3ve (pronounced **“eve”**) botnet is an example of a cyberattack where criminals did not directly steal from people.

Instead, they attacked a **business model**.

The main lesson:

> Attackers do not always hack computers to destroy them. Sometimes they hack computers to create fake activity and make illegal money.

---

# 1. What Is a Botnet?

A **botnet** is a group of infected computers controlled by an attacker.

Think of it like this:

A normal computer:

> Owner controls the computer.

A botnet computer:

> Owner thinks they control the computer, but the attacker secretly gives instructions.

The infected computers are called **bots** or **zombies**.

---

# 2. How Did 3ve Make Money?

The attackers targeted the **digital advertising industry**.

Normally:

```text
Company pays for advertisement
          ↓
Real people see the advertisement
          ↓
Company gains customers
```

The company expects real human attention.

3ve changed the process:

```text
Company pays for advertisements
          ↓
Fake users view advertisements
          ↓
Attackers collect money
```

The views looked real, but they were generated by machines.

---

# 3. How Did They Create Fake Views?

The attackers first infected computers.

They used methods like malicious email attachments.

The process:

```text
Email attachment
        ↓
User opens file
        ↓
Malware installs
        ↓
Computer becomes part of botnet
        ↓
Attacker sends commands
```

The victim may not notice anything unusual.

---

# 4. Why Did They Need Hundreds of Thousands of Computers?

One computer creating fake views would be easy to detect.

Imagine:

A single computer watches the same advertisement 10,000 times.

The advertiser would think:

> "Something is wrong."

But thousands of infected computers from different locations look more natural.

It is like a criminal hiring thousands of fake people to enter a voting system.

The numbers appear real.

---

# 5. Why Did They Send Traffic to Fake Websites?

The attackers created websites they controlled.

The system looked like:

```text
Infected computers
          ↓
Visit fake websites
          ↓
Advertisements appear
          ↓
Fake views generated
          ↓
Advertising money collected
```

They created both sides of the fraud:

* the audience,
* the websites receiving the advertisements.

---

# 6. Why Was It Difficult to Detect?

Because the traffic came from many locations.

A company saw:

* different IP addresses,
* different computers,
* different regions.

It looked like:

> "Many real people are viewing our ads."

But behind the scenes:

> "Many infected machines are following the attacker's instructions."

---

# 7. First-Principles Cybersecurity Lessons

## Lesson 1: Cybercrime follows money

Attackers ask:

> "Where is value created?"

In this case:

Value = advertising views.

So they created fake views.

---

## Lesson 2: Trust in data can be attacked

Companies trusted:

> "More views means more customers."

Attackers manipulated the measurement.

The data looked correct, but the reality was false.

---

## Lesson 3: A computer can become a tool against its owner

The victims were not the main financial targets.

Their computers became resources used to attack others.

---

# Comparing 3ve With Other Malware

| Malware    | Main Goal                               |
| ---------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Stuxnet    | Damage physical systems                 |
| Emotet     | Steal information and spread            |
| WannaCry   | Lock files for ransom                   |
| DarkHotel  | Spy on targeted users                   |
| Fireball   | Control browsing and collect data       |
| 3ve Botnet | Generate fraudulent advertising revenue |

---

# The Deeper Lesson

3ve teaches an important cybersecurity principle:

> In the digital world, control over many ordinary computers can become a powerful weapon.

The attackers did not need to own millions of machines.

They only needed to secretly control enough of them to make fake activity appear real.
